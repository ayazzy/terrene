# Terrene customer discovery

This document separates commercial hypotheses from evidence. Desk research, technical feasibility and product intuition are not validated demand.

## Current wedge hypothesis

Early-stage site screening and diligence for land-intensive development, potentially beginning with UK renewables and infrastructure such as utility-scale solar and battery energy storage systems (BESS).

This is a working go-to-market hypothesis, not a commitment to make renewables Terrene's permanent identity.

## Why it appears plausible

- Site selection and early diligence combine costly land, environmental, planning and infrastructure questions.
- Teams repeatedly assemble fragmented public and private evidence under time pressure.
- False positives, late constraints and poorly documented assumptions can create expensive delay or abandoned work.
- Terrene's existing versioned evidence, spatial relationships, explicit unknowns and reproducible reasoning are technically relevant to this class of decision.

These points establish plausibility only. They do not prove budget, urgency, workflow fit, willingness to change tools or willingness to pay.

## Unvalidated assumptions

- Early screening is painful and frequent enough to support a paid product.
- Developers, investors or advisers own the problem and budget rather than delegating it entirely to consultants.
- Faster evidence assembly changes which sites advance, not merely report presentation.
- The initial connected public sources cover enough of the workflow to create value before private/paid data integrations.
- Users will trust deterministic, inspectable evidence and unknowns inside their decision process.
- A solar/BESS entry point provides repeatable learning that transfers to broader infrastructure or land workflows.
- Portfolio comparison is more valuable than a single-site report.
- Integration, export and audit requirements can be met without reproducing every incumbent GIS workflow.

## Customer profiles to interview

- UK utility-scale solar/BESS development leads and site-originators.
- Development managers responsible for planning and environmental diligence.
- Investment or acquisition teams evaluating project pipelines.
- Environmental/planning consultancies performing early desk studies.
- Land agents and technical advisers involved before detailed surveys.
- Grid/infrastructure developers with comparable land-screening workflows.

## Workflow questions

- What decision is being made, by whom, and at what stage?
- What triggers a site screen and how many occur per month or quarter?
- Which sources, internal evidence and specialists are used today?
- Where do delays, rework, false positives or missed constraints occur?
- Which unknowns are acceptable at screening stage, and which block progression?
- How are source date, coverage, evidence lineage and assumptions recorded?
- What changes between an initial screen, acquisition diligence and planning submission?
- Which outputs must enter an investment memo, consultant brief, GIS package or audit trail?
- What would a useful scenario test change in the decision?
- What is the cost of the current workflow and of a poor decision?
- Who controls budget, procurement, security review and data licensing?
- What evidence would be required to trust or reject a Terrene result?
